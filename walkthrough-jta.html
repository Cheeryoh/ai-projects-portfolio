Follow the evidence, then make the decision.

Define what deserves testing

A draft JTA leads to a practitioner survey. Simulated task ratings, role segments and uncertainty support proposed domain weights. Humans decide what enters the final occupational model.

Measure distinct questions

Twelve simulated SMEs make three rounds of Modified Angoff judgments. Candidate responses support CTT diagnostics. IRT appears only after sufficient observations and model checks, with uncertainty and publication review.

Maintain what was launched

Versioned forms and anchors retain item provenance. Monitoring triggers investigations from response evidence. Reviewed successors can receive a separate synthetic pilot; original scores remain unchanged.

The operational incident

A procedure changes. An item starts measuring the wrong expectation.

75.3% → 20.3% correct

Item-061, form A-1, before and after a fictional thermal-response procedure release. Each complete-cohort window contains 2,000 simulated responses.

Submit synthetic arrivals one batch at a time. Each submission calculates CTT health screens and persists new investigations. The first four batches have insufficient post-release evidence; batch five opens the release-drift incident, preserving the evidence available at that point. A six-step workflow checks cohorts, traces item-to-task evidence, compares procedure versions and considers alternative explanations. Its recommendation awaits a human disposition. It cannot silently change the passing standard or re-score candidates.

The feed is explicitly synthetic, not a live exam-delivery connection. Stored report replay is also available. Investigation services are deterministic analysis workflows. A bounded model capture separately synthesizes the complete-cohort findings into a cited report. Three model role-plays demonstrate candidate behavior. An optional recipe scales twelve captured model-proposed archetypes statistically; none of these captures establishes credential validity.
